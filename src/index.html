WWCP POI in five minutes

Roaming protocols pass lists of charging stations and tariffs from server to server. Nobody can prove later what was published when, and an app shows whatever some server tells it. WWCP POI turns the slowly changing data of e-mobility — charging pools, stations, EVSEs, connectors, energy meters with their public keys, tariffs, manufacturers, transparency software — into a signed, versioned history: an app checks a charging station and its price before charging begins, from one QR code, and can still prove it months later.

1 · Trust from one QR code §8.6

The trust anchor pins the thumbprints of the identity keys of the roaming network operator and its quorum: several companies may hold its keys together, e.g. 2 of 3. Its key statement is signed by that quorum; each holder certifies its own signing keys. No single key, and no server, is trusted on its own.

2 · Versions, change sets, owners §2–4

A version of a network is one JSON document with three ETags: SHA-256 of its canonical JSON, of its deterministic CBOR, and the root of its effective tree. A change set leads from one version to the next and is signed by the owners of what it changes: a charging station operator signs its own pools and tariffs, the roaming network operator decides who is a member.

3 · Commits §5

A commit is a change set applied to its parent: its identification is the hash of its content, and the operator's quorum signs it. Every verifier applies the change set again and must reach exactly the ETags the commit names — byte for byte, whether the server runs C# or this page runs TypeScript.

4 · Templates and the effective tree §8–9

Templates give a thousand EVSEs the same connectors and power once. The effective content resolves them; its hash tree has a Merkle tree (RFC 9162) per child array. A station proof is the network pruned to one station, checked with some dozen hashes and one signature per holder.

5 · Checkpoints and witnesses §10

A checkpoint is the Merkle tree of the commits since the last one. Independent witnesses follow the history commit by commit and sign it; a witness never signs two checkpoints of one sequence, so a server showing different histories to different people is caught. Checkpoints are also where newcomers join and old commits are archived.

6 · Epochs and prices §11–12

When algorithms must change, the final checkpoint closes an epoch and names the next profiles; every server derives the same genesis. Prices that change by the minute are price statements of the charging station operator, chained per EVSE and anchored in checkpoints — the evidence of a charging session.

What this page checks, and where you see it

  • Trust — the key statement against the trust anchor, the certificates of the signing keys, and how the keys move on through the history (key succession, epochs).
  • History — every commit: its identification, its parent, its change set applied again with the signatures of its owners, the ETags it leads to, the quorum of the operator; every checkpoint with its commits tree, the operator and the witnesses; the price statements checkpoints name.
  • Network — any version, as stored or effective: where each value comes from (its own, or a template), the commits that changed an entity, and its path through the effective tree up to the root the commit names.
  • Proofs & prices — station proofs as an app checks them, price statements, proofs of commits and price statements in checkpoints, attestations, the archive, and the evidence of a charging session.
